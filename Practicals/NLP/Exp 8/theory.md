Morphology
Morphology is the study of the internal structure and formation of words. In NLP, morphological analysis breaks words into their smallest meaningful units called morphemes.
For example:
unbelievable → un + believe + able
Here:

- un- = prefix
- believe = root
- -able = suffix
  Morpheme
  A morpheme is the smallest meaningful or grammatical unit of a word.
  Examples:
  play, un-, -ing, -ed, -ness
  Morphemes can be:
- Free Morphemes: Can exist independently, such as book, run, happy.
- Bound Morphemes: Must be attached to another word, such as un-, -ed, -ness.
  Root and Affix
  The root carries the main meaning of a word.
  teacher → teach + er
  Here, teach is the root.
  An affix is added to a root to modify its meaning. A prefix occurs before the root, while a suffix occurs after it.
  Inflectional Morphology
  Inflectional morphology changes the grammatical form of a word without changing its basic meaning or word class.
  Examples:
- cat → cats
- play → played
- run → running
- big → bigger
  Derivational Morphology
  Derivational morphology creates a new word or meaning by adding prefixes or suffixes.
  Examples:
- teach → teacher
- happy → happiness
- kind → unkind
- write → rewrite
  Importance and Applications
  Morphological analysis helps reduce different word forms and identify grammatical information. It is useful in:
- Search engines
- Machine translation
- Text classification
- Information retrieval
- Sentiment analysis
- Question answering
